Critiquing Access at the North York Central Library

A critical access review of the Toronto Public Library’s second-largest branch: what works, what only looks accessible, and what would have to change.

This report checks a crowd-sourced accessibility listing against the building it describes, then asks a harder question than “can you get in?” — can you stay, concentrate, and use the place without having to ask for help.

Verdict: Partially Accessible. Some parts of the branch are barrier-free and others are not, so under Access Now’s own definitions this building is partially accessible, not accessible.

Why access matters at a public library

Every person in Canada has an equal right to take part in public life. The Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms guarantees equal protection and equal benefit of the law without discrimination based on mental or physical disability (Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms, 1982, s. 15(1)). Rioux (2009, p. 205, as cited in La Monica, 2026, “Introduction to Human Rights and Disability” section, para. 2) adds that equal rights mean each person’s needs matter equally and must guide how societies are planned.

What this report does

The North York Central Library is listed on Access Now, a crowd-sourced app that tags places with accessibility features. During a one-hour visit on 29 September 2026, and several times after, I checked that listing against the building.

Most of what the listing claims is true. Two things are not, and the gap between the tag and the room is where this report starts.
