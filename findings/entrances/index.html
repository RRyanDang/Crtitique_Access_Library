Entrances and Movement

Where the building follows Universal Design — and the one route everything depends on.

Nobody has to ask

The entrances reflect Equitable Use, since every visitor uses the same automatic doors (Hamraie, 2017, as cited in La Monica, 2026, “The 7 Principles of Universal Design” section, para. 2). Nobody has to ask, the course’s contrast between reactive accommodation and accessibility built in (La Monica, 2026, “Accommodation vs Accessibility” section, para. 3).

Three more principles, met

Wide pathways and spaced furniture reflect Size and Space for Approach and Use, braille on the elevator buttons and clear sightlines from glass walls and bright lighting support Perceptible Information, and the two-height water fountain reflects Flexibility in Use.

Vertical access shows the contrast

A 2019 news video reports that the $40 million Hunters Point Library in Queens was criticized because its fiction section is reachable only by stairs across three levels (CBS New York, 2019, 0:18–0:30), so a large budget did not prevent a flaw that shuts out patrons.

Here, one interior elevator serves all five floors, so every collection is reachable by the same route, which is built better. Nonetheless, an outage would cut off every upper floor, so I treat this as a strength with a risk rather than full access.

Key takeaway

Four of the seven Universal Design principles are met at the entrance and on the floor plan. Vertical access depends on a single point of failure.
