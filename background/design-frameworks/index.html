Design Frameworks

The standards this building is measured against.

Universal Design

Four of the seven principles of Universal Design do most of the work in this report:

  • Equitable Use — everyone uses the same entrance, not a separate one (Hamraie, 2017, as cited in La Monica, 2026, “The 7 Principles of Universal Design” section, para. 2).
  • Size and Space for Approach and Use — enough room to approach, enter and turn.
  • Perceptible Information — information available in more than one sense.
  • Flexibility in Use — more than one way to use the same thing.

Proactive, not on request

Jaulus (2025, p. 26) argues that accommodation on request is not enough and that support should be proactive as well as reactive. That distinction frames this report: a space is more inclusive when nobody has to ask.

Timing and mismatch

Pereyra (2016, p. 164) reports a design team leader’s view that the earlier accessibility enters the design cycle, the more integrated it will be, and that disability is a mismatch between user and design, not a fault in the person.

Beyond the body

Deep Accessibility asks whether a space works for the senses, not only the body (Deep Accessibility, 2013, as cited in La Monica, 2026, “Access and Accessibility” section, para. 3). It is the framework that makes an elevator chime a design decision rather than a detail.
