About the Library

Five floors, one elevator, a mall entrance and a subway connection — and a building that is supposed to be open to everyone.

The building

The North York Central Library sits at 5120 Yonge St in Toronto, a five-floor branch of the Toronto Public Library. It connects to a mall, whose elevators link it to the parking lot and the subway. It is the second largest branch in the system, and students study there often for its space and study rooms.

Why a library

A public library is meant to be open to everyone, so its accessibility is a question of belonging as well as of getting through the door. Lid (2016) reads Lefebvre’s right to the city as a right to take part in urban life, not just to visit it, and ties access to the built environment to equal citizenship. A library is exactly such a space (Lid, 2016, Introduction section, paras. 2–3).

How this review was done

I visited for one hour on 29 September 2026, and returned several times after. On each visit I checked the building against the claims made in its Access Now listing: entrances, pathways, elevators, water fountains, washrooms and study spaces. This is an observational review by one student, not an audit, and it reflects what was there on those days.

Key takeaway

A library is public space in the fullest sense, so access to it is a question of equal citizenship rather than of convenience.
